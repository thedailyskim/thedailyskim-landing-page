<section aria-labelledby="p1" class="part"><span class="num">01</span><h2 id="p1">The big read</h2>
<article class="story" id="s1">
<figure><img alt="Falling chart on a screen; file photo." height="720" loading="eager" src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1638481826540-7710b13f7d53?fm=jpg&amp;fit=crop&amp;w=1280&amp;h=720&amp;q=80" width="1280"/></figure>
<h3>The S&amp;P 500 and Dow Slide Into a Correction on a Rough Friday</h3>
<p><a href="https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-c10b797a17c2acf37924d834efebe135">The S&amp;P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39 on Friday</a>, the AP reports. That put it more than 10% below its Oct. 6 record, a level investors call a correction. The Nasdaq fell 5.3% and is now down for the year.</p><p><a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/stocks-enter-correction-territory-ai-fears/">The Dow fell 1.9% and also entered a correction</a>, CBS News reports. Chipmakers led losses on worries about slower data center building and tighter export rules. <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/sp-500-correction-market-sell-off.html">Wharton professor Jeremy Siegel told CNBC</a> the drop resets expectations after three years of double-digit returns.</p></article>

<figure><img alt="Network switch with cables; file photo." height="720" loading="lazy" src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1682559736721-c2e77ff4c650?fm=jpg&amp;fit=crop&amp;w=1280&amp;h=720&amp;q=80" width="1280"/></figure>
<h3>Hackers Hit the Agriculture Department as the FBI Probes a Database Breach</h3>
<p><a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/national-security/agriculture-department-breached-hackers-sources-say-rcna610497">The Agriculture Department was hit by a cyberattack this week</a>, according to two former USDA officials who spoke to NBC News. A system the agency uses to pay farmers was breached, officials said.</p><p><a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/justice-department/fbi-investigating-potential-breach-crime-database-rcna610536">The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center</a>, two people briefed told NBC News. The system shares law enforcement information nationwide. The FBI had no comment as of Friday night, NBC reported.</p></article>

<figure><img alt="Gloved hand holding a syringe; file photo." height="720" loading="lazy" src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1746017090180-ebb14a589639?fm=jpg&amp;fit=crop&amp;w=1280&amp;h=720&amp;q=80" width="1280"/></figure>
<h3>A Young Child Dies of Measles in Virginia, the State's First Death This Year</h3>
<p><a href="https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/news/public-relations-contacts/2026-news-releases/vdh-1st-measles-death-2026/">The Virginia Department of Health said a child aged 0 to 4 died</a> after testing positive for measles. It is the state's first measles-associated death in 2026. The department cited a recent increase in measles activity.</p><p><a href="https://abcnews.com/Health/child-measles-dies-virginia-marking-states-1st-measles/story?id=138330916">ABC News reports it is the 12th measles-associated death</a> reported by states this year. The CDC has logged 5,838 confirmed cases in 2026, ABC reports. Virginia officials say two MMR doses are about 97% effective.</p></article>

<h3>A Judge Approves Bayer's Roundup Cancer Settlement of Up to $7.25 Billion</h3>
<p><a href="https://apnews.com/article/bayer-monsanto-roundup-weedkiller-cancer-settlement-075b547ed70802082466ce1fb08773d0">Bayer will pay up to $7.25 billion over two decades</a> to settle claims that Roundup caused lymphoma, the AP reports. A St. Louis judge approved the deal Friday. Bayer denies that Roundup causes cancer.</p><p><a href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/court-approves-7-25-billion-bayer-settlement-over-claims-roundup-weed-killer-caused-cancer">Average payouts could range from $10,000 to $165,000</a>, depending on use, age at diagnosis and severity, PBS reports, citing the AP. Some patient lawyers say the payouts are too small. Others say it beats getting nothing.</p></article>

Prosecutors said he lit a fire on Jan. 1, 2025, that later flared into the Palisades Fire.</p><p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/pacific-palisades-fire-retrial-jonathan-rinderknecht-rcna610336">The Palisades Fire killed 12 people</a> and destroyed over 6,000 homes and other structures, NBC News reports. It caused $25 billion in damage, a top federal prosecutor said. NBC calls it the most destructive wildfire in Los Angeles history. The verdict gives a legal answer on who started it.</p><p><strong>What each side says.</strong> <a href="https://abcnews.com/US/man-accused-starting-blaze-palisades-fire-found-guilty/story?id=138333631">Prosecutors said phone location data placed him 30 feet from the fire</a>, ABC News reports. They said he was angry and frustrated in the days before. Defense attorney Steve Haney cast doubt on the case, ABC reports. A defense expert argued the fire began somewhere else.</p><p><strong>What comes next.</strong> <a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/pacific-palisades-fire-retrial-jonathan-rinderknecht-rcna610336">Sentencing is set for March 17</a>, NBC News reports. Rinderknecht faces a minimum of five years and up to 40 years in prison, prosecutors said. He was convicted on both federal counts, NBC reports. Those are destruction of property by fire and arson affecting interstate commerce.</p></section>
