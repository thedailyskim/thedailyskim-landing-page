A SNAP Proposal Could Remove Benefits From 6.3 Million People

A proposed Agriculture Department rule would restrict broad-based automatic eligibility for SNAP food assistance. AP reports the administration estimates 6.3 million people could lose benefits. The proposal has a public-comment period and would not take full effect for about two years.

CBS News reports critics warn of increased food insecurity. AP says 1.8 million children could lose automatic free-meal eligibility, not necessarily meals. AP reports nearly 97% would likely qualify through another route. The proposal is not an immediate benefits cancellation.

Scott Colom Wins Mississippi’s Senate Race in an Upset

Democrat Scott Colom defeated Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith after days of ballot counting. AP reports he becomes Mississippi's first Black U.S. senator since Reconstruction. Hyde-Smith called Colom to concede and congratulated the prosecutor in a video message.

NBC News reports Democrats will hold at least 55 Senate seats next year, with Alaska still uncalled. Colom campaigned against tariffs and the Iran war. His victory adds a new state-level result to the broader congressional shift already projected after Election Day.

Iran Warns of Wider Retaliation as Israel Readies a Strike

Iran warned of wider retaliation as Israel reportedly stepped up preparations for a possible strike. Al Jazeera reports the Israeli security cabinet's direction was reported by public broadcaster KAN. It was not confirmation that an attack had begun.

Iran's security chief said bases used in a new attack could become targets, including locations beyond West Asia. Al Jazeera reports Tehran rejected a U.S. plan to transfer enriched uranium abroad. Diplomatic proposals and military preparations remained distinct from a final agreement or confirmed operation.

Jensen Huang Resigns From Nvidia as Colette Kress Takes Over

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive Thursday, ending uncertainty over whether he would stay. Reuters reports finance chief Colette Kress was named interim CEO. The departure followed reports of a second relationship involving a company executive.

The change leaves the chipmaker navigating a leadership transition during intense scrutiny of the AI industry. CNBC reports Nvidia shares fell sharply following the news. Kress's appointment is interim. Reuters reports Nvidia will consider internal and external candidates for the permanent role.

Why the US Could Lose Its Measles-Free Status This Month

What happened. An independent Pan American Health Organization panel is reviewing whether measles has become endemic again in several countries. Al Jazeera reports the United States, Mexico and Guatemala are among those at risk of losing elimination status. The United States received that designation in 2000. A review is not itself a final decision.

Why it matters. Elimination means sustained local transmission has been interrupted, not that imported cases never occur. A country can lose the designation after a virus strain spreads continuously for at least 12 months. Al Jazeera explains the review concerns that threshold. Vaccination remains central to preventing outbreaks, regardless of what an international status label says.

What each side says. Researchers describe a possible loss of status as a setback for public health, rather than a surprising outcome. Al Jazeera quotes epidemiologist Matt Ferrari on that distinction. Other experts emphasize the pressure outbreaks put on health systems. The independent panel must assess transmission evidence before recommending any change to a country's designation.

What comes next. The panel's meeting runs through Friday, after which its recommendations go to PAHO Director Jarbas Barbosa. Al Jazeera reports public announcements are expected early next week. Experts will examine genetic sequencing, vaccination coverage and outbreak surveillance. Case totals alone do not determine whether transmission has continued long enough to change a country's status.
