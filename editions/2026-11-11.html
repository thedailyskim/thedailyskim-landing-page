Five States Join the Hims & Hers Privacy Case

Five states joined a Federal Trade Commission lawsuit accusing Hims & Hers of unlawful business practices. Reuters reports the allegations include sharing sensitive health data and making subscriptions difficult to cancel. These are allegations in a lawsuit, not findings by a court.

The complaint also challenges billing and cancellation practices. According to Reuters, Hims & Hers called the allegations flawed and said it would defend itself. The case puts health-data handling and online subscription consent under renewed scrutiny.

Anthropic Adds Meetings and Spreadsheets to Its Workplace Push

Anthropic introduced Claude Meetings, expanding its push into everyday office work. TechCrunch reports the tool turns discussions into structured plans and tasks. The rollout accompanies Claude Sheets and Whiteboards as the company competes in workplace software.

The tools connect meeting notes, planning and spreadsheet work rather than leaving each task in isolation. TechCrunch describes a broader productivity strategy challenging established office suites. Businesses still need to assess access, accuracy and sensitive-data handling before relying on AI-generated work.

Ukraine and Russia Agree in Principle to a Prisoner Exchange

Russia and Ukraine agreed in principle to a large prisoner exchange as two days of Abu Dhabi talks concluded. AP reports the U.S.-mediated discussions produced no major breakthrough on ending the war. Officials had not finalized the exchange's size.

Territory, postwar security guarantees and Europe's role remained obstacles to a settlement. AP reports U.S. envoys planned to visit Moscow next, then meet Ukraine's president in Dublin. A proposed humanitarian exchange is not a final peace agreement.

Stocks Rebound as the Nasdaq Gains 1.6%

U.S. stocks finished higher Wednesday as technology shares recovered. CNBC reports the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.60% to 25,466.19. The S&P 500 rose 1.31% to 7,619.38, while the Dow added 254.60 points to 52,486.49.

CoreWeave jumped nearly 25% after better-than-expected results, while Nvidia rose 2.9%. CNBC reports bond markets were closed for Veterans Day. The equity rebound did not erase the inflation pressures confronting households or determine the Federal Reserve's next decision.

Why the Fed Is Again Talking About Higher Rates

What happened. Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said he leaned toward a quarter-point rate increase in December. His official remarks also left room for a larger move if the data justified one. Waller was describing his own outlook, not announcing a committee decision. The Fed's next action remained unsettled as markets assessed persistent inflation.

Why it matters. Higher policy rates can keep borrowing costs elevated for households and businesses. Reuters reports consumer prices rose briskly for a third consecutive month in October. That complicates the balance between containing inflation and protecting growth. A single official's speech does not automatically change credit-card rates, mortgages or the interest paid on savings.

What each side says. Waller argued that inflation risks justified considering further tightening. CNBC reports he favored a measured increase while remaining open to a larger one. His comments were made before the latest consumer-price release. Market investors can disagree about the likely path, but trading expectations are not commitments from the Federal Open Market Committee.

What comes next. Officials will assess incoming inflation and economic data before the December meeting. CNBC reports stocks rebounded Wednesday even as investors weighed higher rates. Consumers should distinguish a market rally from a policy announcement. The important next development is the committee's actual decision and explanation, not an assumption that one speech settles the outcome.
