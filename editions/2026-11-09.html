Nvidia Opens a Board Inquiry as China Rejects Spying Allegations

Nvidia’s board announced an independent investigation Sunday into reports that CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by foreign intelligence. AP reports that Huang will remain CEO during the review. The board said it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.

China’s Foreign Ministry rejected the allegations Monday, calling them unsubstantiated. AP reports that Huang acknowledged an intimate relationship with a business executive. The New York Times reported that U.S. intelligence identified the woman as gathering information for Beijing. That allegation remains the subject of the company’s inquiry.

An Experimental Treatment Helps Injured Mouse Hearts Repair

A new mouse study offers an early route toward repairing heart muscle after a heart attack. Cambridge researchers report that temporary mRNA treatment stimulated heart cells to divide. Treated mice pumped about 10% more blood per heartbeat than mice receiving a mock treatment, according to the university.

The treatment temporarily activates proteins called Myc and cyclin T1. The study in Nature Cardiovascular Research describes repair and improved function in mice. Researchers still need studies in larger animals and then humans. These findings do not establish a safe or effective treatment for people with heart failure.

Climate Talks Open With Pressure to Turn Promises Into Action

COP31 opened Monday in Antalya, Turkey, with delegates under pressure to implement climate commitments after another year of destructive weather. AP reports that Turkey is hosting while Australia leads negotiations. The conference runs through Nov. 20, with financing for poorer countries among the central disputes.

The energy debate also concerns how quickly countries can replace fossil fuels with electricity from cleaner sources. AP reports that outdated grids are obstructing renewable energy deployment. Installing generation alone is not enough when networks cannot absorb the supply. The talks face both diplomatic disagreements and physical infrastructure constraints.

Egypt Investigates a Train Collision That Killed 7 Near Cairo

2 trains collided Sunday in al Ayyat, south of Cairo. AP reports that 7 people were killed and at least 125 were injured, citing Egyptian authorities. Investigators arrived Sunday night. An initial inspection indicated that a moving train struck the rear of another train standing on the same track.

Rescuers and nearby residents pulled passengers from damaged carriages while emergency teams worked to clear the wreckage. Al Jazeera reported more than 125 injured and said the president ordered an urgent investigation. The reports do not establish responsibility for the collision.

Why AI Developers Are Searching for Used Turbines and Retired Jet Engines

What happened. Utilities and data center developers are searching for used gas turbines and engines adapted from aircraft. Reuters reports that delivery discussions for new U.S. equipment now center on 2030 and 2031. Its Monday analysis draws on brokers, service companies and suppliers. Developers are also reviving idle machinery rather than waiting for new units to arrive.

Why it matters. The shortage reaches beyond technology companies to utilities that need more generating capacity. Reuters cites worldwide turbine orders above 100 gigawatts against estimated global production capacity of 60 to 70 gigawatts. Wood Mackenzie puts average U.S. delivery waits at 6 years. Equipment prices have also risen sharply, according to the Tennessee Valley Authority.

What each side says. Equipment suppliers describe a busy secondary market and rising demand for refurbished machines. But analysts question how much relief it can provide. Enverus analyst Noor Qureshi told Reuters that limited surplus equipment is unlikely to offset a meaningful share of delays. Used turbines can also carry uncertain maintenance histories and limited remaining operating life.

What comes next. More manufacturing capacity is planned, but analysts still expect demand to exceed supply in 2028. Developers are assembling combinations of smaller machines and considering gas engines instead of turbines. Reuters reports that air permits and grid access remain separate obstacles. Securing a generator therefore does not by itself resolve every constraint on a project.
