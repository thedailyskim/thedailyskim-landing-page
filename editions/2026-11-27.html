The big read

Black Friday Shoppers Mix Gifts With Everyday Essentials

Black Friday remains the biggest day of the year for foot traffic at physical stores, AP reports, citing Sensormatic Solutions. Consumers spent $82.5 billion online from Nov. 1 to Nov. 22, according to Adobe. Analysts said shoppers are also stocking up on discounted household basics.

The National Retail Federation sees November-December holiday sales rising 4.2% to just above $1 trillion, Reuters reports via NBC News. Much of the gain reflects 3.8% inflation. Chicago shoppers told CBS they are keeping budgets in mind amid affordability concerns.

Storms Leave Hundreds of Thousands Without Power After Thanksgiving

Almost 700,000 customers were still without power early Friday, according to PowerOutage.us, NBC News reports. Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Maryland were hardest hit. That was down from a peak of about 940,000 on Thanksgiving.

NBC News reports 462 flights were canceled Thursday, citing FlightAware. Heavy storms disrupted travel across the country on the holiday, AP reports via Al Jazeera. Central Park had its wettest Thanksgiving on record with 1.82 inches of rain, NBC News reports, citing the National Weather Service.

Stock Futures Jump Ahead of a Shortened Friday Session

S&P 500 futures were up nearly 2% and Nasdaq-100 futures rose 2.8% early Friday, CNBC reported as of 4:12 a.m. ET. Dow futures were up 655 points. The stock market is scheduled to close at 1 p.m. ET Friday.

CNBC said the S&P 500 is up almost 4% week to date. AI-related stocks gave back some gains after Monday. A study spanning 170 years put the probability of an AI-driven bubble as high as 97%, CNBC reports. Two other models estimated about 40%.

New Zealand's Ruling Coalition Falls One Seat Short of a Majority

Official results Friday showed Prime Minister Christopher Luxon fell just short of a parliamentary majority, Reuters reports. His coalition holds 65 seats in the 130-seat parliament. Luxon will seek support from the centrist Opportunity Party, which won seven seats.

Analysts told Reuters a fresh election was unlikely, but talks could run into next year. "Kiwis won't thank us for rushing to an agreement that falls apart," Luxon said. The results offer no clear path to forming a government, Al Jazeera reports.

Why the Gulf Conflict Is Flaring Again

What happened. Two Italians were killed in an alleged attack on Kuwait, Kuwaiti forces said Thursday, CNBC reports. Kuwait's army said falling missile debris after Iranian attacks caused the deaths. Separately, an aerial projectile hit a Saudi refinery in Jazan on Thursday, killing one worker, according to Saudi Arabia's energy ministry.

Why it matters. The Jazan refinery near the Yemeni border can process up to 400,000 barrels of crude a day, Al Jazeera reports. The ministry said the strike caused a fire and temporarily halted some operations. A Saudi-led coalition said Yemen's Houthis attacked it. The group had claimed attacks on Ras Tanura a day earlier.

What each side says. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran had no role in the Kuwait incident, CNBC reports. He called it "suspicious" and offered to help investigate. An Israeli military spokesman said Israel was prepared to strike Iran "at any moment," even without U.S. forces. Trump said on PBS that victory was "at hand."

What comes next. Iran's armed forces chief of staff warned the US and Israel against attacking Iran, Al Jazeera reports. Ali Abdollahi said the navy would deliver a firm response to any aggression. Trump said Washington was still giving Iran "every chance to make a deal," CNBC reports. He said he was unsure Tehran would agree.
