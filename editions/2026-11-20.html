The Senate Passes an Energy Bill That Makes Data Centers Pay for Power

The Senate voted 62-33 on Thursday to speed up permitting for power plants, transmission lines and other energy projects, the AP reports. The bill would also make sure data centers pay for the energy they use. It now goes to the House.

CNBC reports the bill is projected to lower energy costs for consumers while weakening some environmental laws. More than a dozen Democrats, including Chuck Schumer, voted yes. Opponents lamented rollbacks to bedrock environmental protection laws, CNBC reports.

Record Rain Floods Florida as Storms Move Up the Space Coast

Storms shifted north to Florida's Space Coast on Thursday, prompting nearly 100 high-water rescues in Brevard County, NBC News reports, citing the AP. The Fort Lauderdale area got nearly 17 inches of rain in 48 hours, according to preliminary weather service totals.

ABC News reports the St. Johns River near Cocoa rose above record levels. The National Hurricane Center gave the system a 60% chance of becoming tropical or subtropical this weekend. A flood threat stretches from Daytona Beach to Charleston on Saturday.

The Average 30-Year Mortgage Rate Rises to 7.28% After a Four-Week Slide

The average 30-year mortgage rate rose to 7.28% from 7.11%, Freddie Mac said Thursday, according to the AP. It was the first weekly increase since early October. A year ago, the average was 6.26%.

The 15-year rate climbed to 6.66% from 6.45%, ABC News reports. Rates track the 10-year Treasury yield, which was 5.05% at midday Thursday. Signs of hotter inflation have raised the odds of a Fed rate hike in December.

The White House Names a New Cyber Chief After a Week of Outages

The White House named Kirsten Davies as national cyber director on Thursday after widespread service outages, the AP reports. Davies is the Defense Department's chief information officer. She replaces Sean Cairncross.

A source told NBC News Davies will serve in an acting capacity. The post requires Senate confirmation. Officials and some Democrats have said the outages do not appear to be cyberattacks, NBC reports.

The US Sells Its First Deep-Sea Mining Leases, and the Lawsuits Follow

What happened. The Trump administration auctioned two deep-sea mining leases near American Samoa on Thursday, the AP reports. Startup Wetstone U.S. Ventures won both at the $3 million minimum bid each. The 20-year leases are the first of their kind in the U.S. They cover millions of acres of federal seafloor.

Why it matters. The Interior Department says the two areas cover about 31.5 million acres at depths of 4,630 to 20,100 feet. It calls the sale a step for mineral security. Critical minerals are used in electronics and defense systems. Globally, no company has started commercial deep-sea mining, according to the AP.

What each side says. Interior Secretary Doug Burgum called it a historic step for American mineral security, the department said. Earthjustice says a court ruling on Monday made the auction unlawful. Its attorney Chris Eaton said the group will push for the leases to be voided. An industry group expects interest and investment to grow.

What comes next. The leases let Wetstone begin seafloor surveys, the AP reports. Commercial mining would need approval in further environmental reviews. Three lawsuits seek to halt or strike down the sales. The Marine Minerals Administration is considering further auctions near the Northern Mariana Islands, Virginia and Alaska, the AP reports.
