Oil Just Crashed 7%. Apple's New Boss Posted a Record Quarter. And a SpaceX Exec Is Giving Kids 2 Million Shares.

Hopes for Iran peace talks drove oil to its steepest one-day drop in months, and Apple's first quarter under John Ternus set new records. Investors weren't fully sold on either.

The Headlines

Oil Plunged More Than 7% in a Single Day. Here's Why

Brent crude settled down 7.6% at $104.77 a barrel on Monday, and U.S. crude fell 7.4% to $96.46, the biggest one-day drop in months. The selloff followed talks between Iranian and Qatari officials on ending the war that began in late February, after Iran's president said the supreme leader had authorized negotiations. Analysts urged caution after months of false starts, noting that attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz remain a risk.

Sources: Reuters via Investing.com · Reuters via Yahoo Finance

Apple's New CEO Posted a Record Quarter on His First Try

John Ternus's first report as Apple CEO showed September-quarter revenue up 12% to $114.5 billion, a record for that quarter. iPhone sales grew 17% to $57.6 billion, Mac sales rose 24%, and Services hit an all-time high of $31.6 billion. Apple expects holiday-quarter revenue to grow 7% to 10%, but it guided to slimmer profit margins, and Ternus called the jump in memory-chip prices "unprecedented." Shares slipped after hours.

Sources: Fortune · MacRumors

A Sitting U.S. Senator Had a Stroke at His Son's Hockey Game

Connecticut Sen. Chris Murphy, 53, is in the hospital after a stroke, his office said Sunday. Symptoms began Saturday at his son's hockey game, and he was moved to Johns Hopkins for monitoring. His chief of staff said he is awake, alert and responding well, and his office expects a full recovery. Senators from both parties sent well wishes. Murphy is not on the ballot this week.

Sources: AP via PBS NewsHour

The Context

Europe's Gen Z Is Marching. What Started It?

What happened

France saw a third straight weekend of protests on Oct. 31, with the government estimating 320,000 people nationwide, down from 550,000 on Oct. 17. In Germany, a youth group called "Gen Z regelt" held rallies in more than 30 cities, and some turned violent in Frankfurt, Cologne and Berlin.

Why it matters

The movement began with French high school students angry about crumbling schools, teacher shortages and a university admissions system they call unfair. Unions and climate activists have since joined. German marchers want spending on education, housing and climate, and they oppose bringing back military conscription.

What to watch

French schools reopened Monday after the autumn break, and student groups and unions have called nationwide protests for later this week. German officials have promised to prosecute the rioters.

Sources: AP via ABC News · ABC Australia
