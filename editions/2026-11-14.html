Supreme Court Agrees to Weigh Trump's Asylum Ban at the Southern Border

The Supreme Court agreed Friday to consider President Trump's ban on asylum at the southern border. AP reports lower courts found it illegal to block asylum applications. The order called the border situation an "invasion." Arguments are expected in spring, with a ruling by the end of June.

Reuters reports a federal judge ruled against the policy in July 2025, and an appeals court upheld that ruling in April. Solicitor General D. John Sauer wrote that border encounters have fallen 93% since the order took effect. Immigrant advocates say crossings were already falling before Trump took office.

Airlines Dig Out After the Longest Nationwide Ground Stop in 25 Years

More than 2,000 flights were canceled Saturday as airlines worked through the fallout from Friday's nationwide ground stop. AP reports officials blamed new technology that overloaded antiquated air traffic equipment. The stop lasted more than four hours and was lifted around 8:15 p.m. ET Friday.

CBS News reports Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said overloaded routers produced inconsistent data. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no cyberattack and no link to the Microsoft outage. Duffy warned of more outages unless Congress funds new systems. He expects flights to be back up for Thanksgiving travel.

All Aboard Survive After a Tanker Slams Into a New Orleans River Cruise Boat

A tanker that apparently lost its steering hit the docked American Heritage river cruise boat in New Orleans on Friday. AP reports all 180 people aboard survived after the boat flipped and many plunged into the Mississippi River. The mayor said 32 people went to the hospital, none with critical injuries.

CBS News reports the Coast Guard is investigating an apparent loss of steering. NTSB investigators planned to drive to New Orleans because of the nationwide flight disruption. The tanker, Atlantic Asphalt, is owned by a subsidiary of China State Shipbuilding Corporation, according to MarineTraffic.

Alaska Elects Its First Democratic Governor Since 1998

AP reports Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins won the Alaska governor race, the first Democrat elected to the office since 1998. He defeated three Republicans, including Trump-endorsed Bernadette Wilson. Democrats will now hold the governor's office in 30 states.

NBC News reports Kreiss-Tomkins won a majority on the first ballot, so ranked choice counting was not needed. At 37, he will be the youngest governor in Alaska history. He campaigned on school funding, lower energy costs and a pause on new data centers. He takes office Dec. 7.

Why States Can Wait Until July to Drop People Over Medicaid Work Rules

What happened. The Trump administration told states Friday they can wait until July before removing people from Medicaid for missing a work requirement. CMS announced an optional hold harmless period through June 30, 2027. The requirement still takes effect nationally on Jan. 1. States using the delay must still check eligibility but cannot drop people solely for noncompliance.

Why it matters. KFF says the 2025 reconciliation law requires 44 states, including DC, to apply work rules to certain adults. Many enrollees must work, volunteer or join a work program 80 hours a month, or attend school half-time. AP reports critics say most affected enrollees already work and fear the paperwork. Exceptions cover people over 64 and parents of children under 14.

What each side says. CMS official Dan Brillman said states have made real progress and were clear about what it takes to get this right. Kathy Hempstead of the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation told AP the extra time should mean fewer people lose coverage over administrative failures. The National Association of Medicaid Directors said state leaders appreciate the added flexibility.

What comes next. It is not yet clear which states will use the extra time, AP reports. Democrats, who take control of both chambers of Congress in January, have said they want to roll back the requirements. A Boston federal court recently ruled that medically frail people should not be bound by the rules. CMS urges states to verify work through existing data first.
