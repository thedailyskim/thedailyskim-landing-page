The FDA Orders a Rare Vision Loss Warning on GLP 1 Drugs

The FDA is requiring all GLP 1 drug labels to warn of a rare eye condition called NAION. The agency says the condition can cause sudden, painless vision loss in one eye. Its analysis of 126 trials found 6.4 cases per 100,000 person years on the drugs, versus 3.8 on comparators.

AP reports the warning covers brands including Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound and Mounjaro. The underlying cause is not understood, the FDA said. The Washington Post reports that studies on the link have reached conflicting conclusions. Novo Nordisk said its own trial analysis found no association, and Lilly said it was evaluating the update.

Lilly’s Weight Loss Pill Cuts Sleep Apnea Episodes in Trials

Eli Lilly said Monday that its oral drug Foundayo significantly reduced sleep apnea severity in two late stage studies. Reuters reports the trial met its main goal and Lilly shares rose over 3%. The program enrolled 712 adults with overweight or obesity and moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea.

Fierce Pharma reports episodes fell 43.8% among patients not using breathing machines, compared with a smaller drop on placebo. Patients also lost about 11% of body weight. Stomach side effects were most common, and more Foundayo patients stopped treatment. Lilly plans to submit the data to the FDA by year end.

Anthropic Retracts IPO Pricing Details Sent to Banks

Details of Anthropic’s planned IPO briefly reached banks late Monday before the company retracted them. Reuters reports the filing showed about 867 million shares at $70 to $80 each. That would raise about $69.3 billion at the top of the range. A follow up email called the pricing details inaccurate without saying what was wrong.

The original filing also listed $26 billion in planned investments from Google, Nvidia, Microsoft and other technology companies. A Reuters markets column says a successful listing is seen as critical for the AI trade. The roadshow was expected to begin Tuesday, with pricing the following week. Anthropic declined to comment.

Novaya Gazeta Closes Its Moscow Newsroom After 33 Years

Russia’s Novaya Gazeta left its Moscow office Monday, citing relentless government pressure. AP describes it as the last major independent Kremlin critic still active inside Russia. Founded in 1993, the paper has seen 6 of its journalists killed, including Anna Politkovskaya. Staff removed their portraits from the newsroom on the final day.

Novaya Gazeta Europe reports that editor Sergey Sokolov insisted this is not goodbye. A new sign over the nameplate said the paper had lived there. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov disputed that the era of independent Russian media is ending. He described Russian media as diverse and competitive.

Deep dive

Why Federally Funded Labs Must Now Buy DNA From Screened Suppliers

What happened. A new White House policy requires federally funded scientists to buy synthetic DNA and benchtop synthesizers only from approved suppliers. Science reports vendors must verify customers and screen orders for dangerous sequences. The policy was released Nov. 5 and replaces a voluntary system. Its requirements phase in over 340 days.

Why it matters. Officials and experts worry that artificial intelligence could make designing dangerous pathogens easier. Factlen reports the policy lowers the screening threshold to orders of 50 nucleotides. The earlier industry protocol screened orders of 200 base pairs or longer. Benchtop machines must also receive verified authorization before printing.

What each side says. Stanford’s Drew Endy called the move from voluntary to verified screening a real step forward. MIT’s Kevin Esvelt told Science that shorter fragments could still be ordered and stitched together. Johns Hopkins expert Gigi Gronvall noted the rules cover only the United States. She also said existing benchtop machines are left out.

What comes next. NIST must issue screening and benchtop standards by day 90 of the timeline. Factlen reports health officials will publish a first list of adherent suppliers by day 160. Endy questioned whether NIST has the money for the work. Esvelt said biosafety officers face added duties without extra funds.
