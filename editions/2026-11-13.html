Microsoft Struggles to Restore Outlook and Teams After a Global Outage

Microsoft is still working to restore Outlook, Teams and other Microsoft 365 services after an outage that began Thursday. AP reports the disruption hit companies and some government operations from Asia to the United States. The company says it has no sign of malicious activity. More than 430 million people use Microsoft 365 apps.

CNBC reports Microsoft saw early signs of stabilization Friday, but full capacity was not restored. Al Jazeera reports Microsoft blamed elevated load after a number of servers became unexpectedly unavailable. The disruption also hit tools that OpenAI and Anthropic rely on. Teams chats and calls have largely kept working.

Trump Threatens to Double Tariffs on Mexico Over a Migrant Caravan

President Trump threatened Thursday to double tariffs on Mexico unless it stops migrants walking north. Reuters reports about 1,500 people, mostly Central Americans, set off Tuesday from Tapachula. Trump wrote that tariffs would be immediately doubled if Mexico failed to act. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the caravan would not enter.

AP reports President Claudia Sheinbaum said no caravan has reached the U.S. border since January 2025. She said one group of about 600 had largely dispersed, and she ruled out force. The threat comes during trade talks. Mexico wants cuts to 10% tariffs on goods outside the USMCA and a 25% truck duty.

Cisco Posts Record Sales as Demand for AI Networking Grows

Cisco reported record quarterly revenue of $18.5 billion, up 24% from a year earlier. Adjusted earnings were $1.40 per share. CNBC reports both figures beat analyst estimates. Cisco raised its forecast for AI infrastructure revenue from the largest cloud companies to $9 billion this fiscal year.

Cisco also lifted its full-year revenue outlook to between $74.8 billion and $75.8 billion. Its adjusted gross margin narrowed to 65.6% from 68.1% a year earlier. CNBC reports shares rose in extended trading Thursday. The stock had gained about 35% this year.

NOAA Sees a 60% Chance of a Record-Strength Pacific Warming Event

NOAA’s Climate Prediction Center said Thursday that El Niño continues to strengthen. Sea surface temperature anomalies topped 4 degrees Celsius in the eastern equatorial Pacific. Forecasters put the chance of a historic event at about 60% this fall and early winter. A strong to very strong El Niño is likely through March.

CBS News reports southern U.S. states should prepare for a stormier winter than usual. The northern part of the continent is likely to see milder weather with less precipitation. The term super El Niño is not official. NOAA and the World Meteorological Organization call this event very strong.

Why a New HUD Rule Could Push Mixed-Status Families Out of Their Homes

What happened. The Department of Housing and Urban Development published a final rule Thursday on tenant eligibility. CBS News reports public housing residents must show proof of citizenship or eligible immigration status. The requirement covers household members of all ages. Since 1995, mixed-status families could live together if subsidies covered only eligible members. The rule takes effect Dec. 14.

Why it matters. A policy institute cited by CBS News estimates about 20,000 mixed-status families, or 80,000 people, could be affected. About 37,000 of them are children, mostly U.S. citizens. Families in all 50 states would feel it. CBS reports experts say these households often pay a higher share of rent because their subsidies are prorated.

What each side says. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said American taxpayers will no longer foot the bill for ineligible tenants. CBS News reports he cited an audit that found nearly 200,000 tenants with incomplete or unverified status. Marie Claire Tran-Leung of the National Housing Law Project said the rule is tearing apart families. She warned HUD will likely face financial shortfalls.

What comes next. The final rule in the Federal Register makes prorated aid temporary while every family member's status is verified. CBS News reports 22 state attorneys general opposed the rule. They warned of significant economic costs to states. California has more than 7,000 affected households and New York about 2,500.
