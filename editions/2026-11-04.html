Your Health Bills and Taxes Enter a New Congressional Debate

The midterm results could reshape debates over household costs, but they do not immediately change anyone's benefits or taxes. CNBC reports Democrats could seek renewed Affordable Care Act subsidies and changes to Medicaid work requirements. Newly elected lawmakers take office in January.

President Donald Trump retains veto power, limiting what the incoming majority can enact alone. CNBC also identifies student-loan rules, housing and retirement savings as possible areas for legislation or oversight. These are potential policy paths, not measures already approved.

CVS Plans to Separate Its Pharmacies From Insurance

CVS Health plans to split into two public companies by the first half of 2028. CNBC reports roughly 9,000 retail pharmacies would separate from the insurer Aetna and pharmacy benefit manager Caremark. The plan follows a board strategic review.

The insurance and benefits company would retain the CVS Health name under CEO David Joyner. According to CNBC, Prem Shah would lead CVS Pharmacy. Existing shareholders would receive stock in both companies. CVS shares closed more than 12% lower Wednesday.

Healthcare and Clean Energy Shares Rally After the Vote

Investors moved into healthcare and clean energy shares after the midterms, while defense and data-center stocks fell. CNBC reports Centene gained 12.6% and HCA Healthcare rose 10.3%. Traders anticipated possible changes to health spending and energy policy.

Lockheed Martin fell 6.9%, while CoreWeave lost 9.9% amid concerns about military spending and restrictions on data-center construction. CNBC lists Microsoft and Apple among major technology winners. These market reactions reflect expectations, not guarantees about legislation or future returns.

England Will Test Blood Screening for Several Cancers

At least 200 English GP practices will offer multi-cancer blood tests in a government-funded trial. The UK health department says more than 20,000 symptomatic patients aged 45 or older will take part. Researchers aim to identify abdominal cancers earlier.

The tests look for signals such as blood proteins and may help doctors decide who needs an urgent referral. The department explains a positive result is not a confirmed cancer diagnosis. The trial will assess performance before any nationwide rollout.

Idaho's Abortion Vote Leaves Its Existing Ban in Place

What happened. Idaho voters rejected Proposition 1, which would have protected abortion access until fetal viability, NBC News projects. The result leaves the state's existing abortion ban in place. That ban took effect in 2022 after the Supreme Court overturned Roe v. Wade. The proposed initiative would have taken effect in 2027 if approved.

Why it matters. The initiative covered more than abortion. Its definition of reproductive freedom included contraception, fertility treatment, miscarriage care and care during and after pregnancy. NBC News reports it would have protected personal healthcare decisions made with a provider. Voters' rejection means those proposed protections do not become state law through this measure.

What each side says. The initiative's text argued that reproductive healthcare belongs to patients in consultation with providers. Idaho's existing law instead preserves the state's abortion restrictions. NBC News describes the proposal's limit as fetal viability, generally around 23 to 24 weeks. It also included an exception after viability for medical emergencies threatening the woman's life.

What comes next. This election result does not create a new statewide abortion right or implement the initiative's proposed 2027 start date. AP also reports Idaho's rejection among the election's reproductive-rights ballot decisions. Patients and providers remain subject to existing law. The projection should not be confused with a claim that every ballot has been counted.
