Tesla Makes Factory Room for Cybercab, Leaving Cybertruck Questions

Tesla plans to wind down Cybertruck production at its Texas factory next year, Elon Musk says. He says the change will make room for more Cybercab vehicles.

That is not yet confirmation of a worldwide shutdown. Musk did not explain whether production would end entirely or move elsewhere, the BBC reports.

The truck launched in 2023 after delays, and sales have disappointed. A 2025 recall covered more than 46,000 vehicles over concerns that exterior trim could detach, according to the BBC's account of its rollout.

Raman Inherits a City Agenda and a Global Sporting Calendar

Nithya Raman will take charge of Los Angeles before it hosts the 2027 Super Bowl and 2028 Summer Olympics. The city council member defeated incumbent Mayor Karen Bass, who conceded Thursday.

Raman campaigned on housing, homelessness and protecting residents from immigration enforcement. Her platform also included preserving Hollywood jobs, road repairs and reviving nightlife.

The city's response to the devastating 2025 fires was a major election issue, the BBC reports. Bass faced criticism over her absence during the emergency and the response to it.

The Typical Cash Buyer Purchasing a First Home Is 54

A record 1 in 10 people buying their first home paid entirely in cash, according to a Realtors report covered by NPR. Those cash buyers had a median age of 54, compared with 38 for buyers using financing.

Inherited wealth helps explain the difference. Among these cash buyers, 28% used an inheritance and 21% used proceeds from inherited property.

Buyers purchasing their first home represented just 22% of all buyers. NPR also describes relatives lending cash for purchases, with some buyers later taking mortgages to repay them.

California Clemency Does Not Make Every Sentence Change Automatic

Governor Gavin Newsom has announced commutations covering 548 people facing execution in California. The intended replacement is life without the possibility of parole.

Some cases still require California Supreme Court approval, The Marshall Project reports. Justices must review commutations for people convicted of multiple felonies, more than half of those covered.

California has not carried out an execution since 2006. Newsom's action does not abolish the death penalty, which prosecutors can still seek under state law.

Pope Leo's Latin America Journey Connects Diplomacy and Local Communities

What happened. Pope Leo XIV has left Rome for a 12 day journey through Uruguay, Argentina and Peru, Vatican News reports. He departed from Fiumicino International Airport on Friday. His first stop is Montevideo, where Friday's schedule includes meetings with government representatives and diplomats. He will also visit Uruguay's president before celebrating Vespers with clergy at the metropolitan cathedral.

Why it matters. Uruguay, the region's most secular country, has not hosted a pope since 1988, according to AP's reporting published by NPR. Its itinerary includes a neighborhood affected by gang violence and Masses in Paysandú and Florida. Leo will later return to Peru, where he worked as a missionary priest and bishop for 2 decades.

What each side says. Vatican communications chief Maria Montserrat Alvar says doctors are confident about Leo's recovery after his Oct. 13 lung cancer surgery. Medical care will be available during the journey. Uruguayan church figures describe enthusiasm for the visit, AP reports. But Catholic studies scholar Andrew Chesnut cautions that a warm welcome need not translate into more people attending church.

What comes next. The Vatican's published schedule runs through Nov. 17, with stops in 10 cities and nearly 40 addresses expected in Spanish. Argentina follows Uruguay, with Buenos Aires, Córdoba and Luján on the itinerary. Peru comes last, including Lima, Chiclayo, Cusco and Pucallpa. Montevideo's welcome ceremony is scheduled for Friday evening in Independence Square.
