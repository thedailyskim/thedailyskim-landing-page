Democrats are projected to win both chambers of Congress in a blow to Trump

Democrats won control of the House and Senate in the Nov. 3 midterms, according to CBS News projections. CBS projected the House at 10:15 p.m. ET and the Senate shortly after 11:30 p.m. ET. Senate races in Alaska and Mississippi were still uncalled on Nov. 4.

Republicans held the Senate 53 to 47 before the vote. CBS now projects Democrats will hold at least 54 Senate seats and between 251 and 268 House seats. Democrats flipped Senate seats in North Carolina, Texas, Ohio, Maine and Iowa.

Trump said he would accept the results and congratulated Democrats. He told CBS he still holds "the ultimate power" of the veto. Gas averaged $4.15 a gallon, the highest Election Day average on record.

Abroad, Iran's parliament speaker praised the vote as a rejection of war, CBS reported. Stock futures fell overnight as investors weighed the risk to the AI and data center boom, NBC News reported.

Iran says four tankers hit mines near the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said four oil tankers were destroyed after hitting naval mines south of the Strait of Hormuz, Middle East Eye reported on Nov. 4. It said the ships used a prohibited route and that it seized two other vessels. The claims have not been independently verified.

Separately, the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency said two projectiles struck a tanker's engine room off Limah, Oman. No casualties were reported. Al Jazeera said the incident could not be linked to Iran's claims.

Iran called Washington's reply to its plan to reopen the strait "unconvincing." Tehran wants the U.S. to lift sanctions and end a naval blockade first. Qatar said both sides remain committed to talks.

Indonesia's president fires his chief of staff over alleged wiretapping

Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto fired his chief of staff, Listyo Sigit Prabowo, on Nov. 4, Al Jazeera reported. Police had arrested him a day earlier over alleged illegal wiretapping. Listyo led the national police for five years before taking the post in October.

Police say three senior generals tapped the phones of the president, ministers, journalists and activists. Investigators seized 11 phones, an iPad and four flash drives, ABC News reported.

A presidential spokesperson said the acts could endanger the nation's leadership. Law minister Yusril Ihza Mahendra said the motive is still unknown. The military deployed more than 20,000 soldiers in Jakarta but did not say if this was linked.

Russian strike on a Ukraine aid center kills 37 as peace talks near

The death toll from a Russian bombing of an aid point in Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia region rose to 37, Reuters reported on Nov. 4. Officials said 59 people were missing. Rescuers were still searching the rubble of a former school in Komyshuvakha.

The school served as a shelter and handed out aid every Monday. President Zelensky said Russian forces bombed it as people arrived, then struck again. Two rescuers and four police officers were among the dead.

Russia said it targeted Ukrainian military units. The strike came a day after a six day evacuation ceasefire for Oleshky expired, RTÉ reported. U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff said both delegations will meet in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10 and 11.

Deep dive

Ethiopia expels Sudanese diplomats as fears of a wider war grow

What happened. Ethiopia ordered six staff members of Sudan's consulate in the border town of Assosa to leave within 48 hours, Reuters reported on Nov. 4. A senior Ethiopian official announced the move without giving a reason. It came days after Sudan filed complaints against Ethiopia with the UN Security Council and the African Union.

Why it matters. Sudan's civil war began in April 2023 and has displaced more than 14 million people, according to UN figures. Sudan says the paramilitary RSF has launched drones from Ethiopian soil. Its sovereign council counted 70 assaults involving 187 drones between Feb. 15 and Oct. 27. Reuters notes the conflicts could spread across the Horn of Africa.

What each side says. Sudan accuses Ethiopia of backing the Rapid Support Forces. Ethiopia accuses Sudan, Egypt and Eritrea of supporting Tigrayan rebels who began an offensive in September. All deny the claims. Sudanese adviser Amjad Farid said Khartoum is making "every possible effort to avoid an open war with Ethiopia." Ethiopia's prime minister's office did not immediately comment.

What comes next. The six consulate staff have 48 hours to leave Assosa, TimesLIVE reported. Sudan's complaints now sit before the UN Security Council and the African Union. UN chief Antonio Guterres has strongly condemned the escalating drone attacks in Sudan. Farid said Sudan will defend its territory while still seeking to avoid war.
