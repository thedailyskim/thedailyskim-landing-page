Wall Street Posts Its Best Week Since April 2025

The S&P 500 rose 1.6% on Friday and gained 5.6% for the week, its biggest weekly gain since April 2025, AP reports. The Dow rose 591 points, or 1.2%. The Nasdaq composite rose 2.3%.

Markets were closed Thursday for Thanksgiving, and Friday was a shortened session, AP reports. Capital Economics warned the rebound may not hold, citing 11 earlier fast 10% drops, CNBC reports. Five of those pullbacks turned into bear markets, the firm said.

OpenAI Countersues Apple Over Trade Secrets

OpenAI filed counterclaims against Apple in California federal court on Friday, CNBC reports. It accused Apple of stealing trade secrets and discouraging hardware suppliers from working on future OpenAI devices.

Apple called the counterclaims "a transparent attempt to distract" from its own lawsuit, CNBC reports. Apple sued OpenAI and two former employees in July, alleging misappropriation of trade secrets, Al Jazeera reports. OpenAI has denied those allegations.

New Car Prices Hit a Record as Reliability Slips

The average new-vehicle transaction price hit a record $51,109 in October, up 2.1% from a year earlier, CNBC reports, citing Kelley Blue Book. Sales of new cars priced from about $28,000 to $38,000 rose 9.6%.

Cars are also becoming less reliable. Owners of 3-year-old models reported an average of 204 problems per 100 vehicles, CNBC reports, citing J.D. Power data as of Feb. 12.

An Explosion Beside an Afghan School Kills Seven, Officials Say

A school explosion in Afghanistan's Kunar province killed seven people Saturday and wounded more than 90, an official told AP. He said the blast appeared to come from a neighboring explosives depot.

Initial information pointed to negligence at the depot, which stored explosives for mining companies, the official said. The Interior Ministry spokesman also blamed negligence, citing initial investigations, Al Jazeera reports. Casualty figures were preliminary.

What Spain's Snap Election Could Change

What happened. Spain votes Sunday in a snap election that could end Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez's eight years in power, CNBC reports. The center-right Popular Party leads the polls. The far-right Vox party could play a strong role. Campaigning wrapped up ahead of the vote, with polls banned in the final five days, Al Jazeera reports.

Why it matters. CNBC reports that housing and ties with the United States are in focus. Sanchez repeatedly clashed with President Donald Trump over NATO spending, Gaza and the use of Spanish bases. A Popular Party-led government could move Spain closer to Washington, CNBC reports. A strong role for Vox could complicate any reset, it adds.

What each side says. "I can't conceive of us losing," Sanchez told radio station SER on Friday, Al Jazeera reports. He said internal data suggested his Socialists would win the most votes. He said left-leaning voters were increasingly mobilising. Public polling before the ban suggested a right-wing advantage, Al Jazeera reports.

What comes next. Polls open Sunday, and the result will show whether Sanchez can clinch re-election with smaller leftist allies, as he hopes. Al Jazeera reports that Europe opens a 12-month electoral cycle that could reshape power within the European Union. Spain's vote will be closely watched, Al Jazeera reports.
