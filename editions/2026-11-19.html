Your Local Pharmacist Could Soon Handle More of Your Checkups

UnitedHealth Group said Wednesday it plans to expand a program that pays independent pharmacists for medical services, Reuters reports. Those services are normally provided in doctors' offices. The insurer pays pharmacists in a national network, subject to state laws.

The company says the model covers flu and strep testing, diabetes and blood pressure support, and medication counseling. It is now open to Community Plan members in seven states, including Florida and Michigan. The insurer says it plans to add more states.

Bird Flu Forces the Biggest US Egg Producer to Cull 1.8 Million Birds

Cal-Maine Foods said Wednesday it will cull about 1.8 million birds after bird flu was found at a Florida facility, CNBC reports. That is 2.7% of its flock. The company has halted production at the site.

Al Jazeera reports eggs average $2.32 a dozen, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, far below the record $6.23 in 2025. About 21.5 million birds have been affected this year. Health officials say the risk to the public remains low.

Europe Offers Ukraine a New Loan It Repays Only If Russia Pays

The European Commission proposed up to EUR 45 billion in new support for Ukraine in 2027 on Wednesday. That would cover about two-thirds of an estimated EUR 70 billion gap. Ukraine would repay the principal only after receiving Russian reparations.

Reuters reports Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Ukraine would only need to repay after Russia paid for war damage. The Commission says up to EUR 25 billion would go to military help. Budget aid would depend on anti-corruption reforms.

Voyager 1 Is Now About a Full Light-Day Away From Earth

NASA calculated that Voyager 1 would reach one light-day from Earth on Wednesday at 5:16 a.m. EST. That is the distance light travels in 24 hours, about 16 billion miles. No other human-made object has gone that far.

Reuters reports the milestone is based on advance computations by NASA, so the probe is believed to have reached it. Voyager 1 launched in 1977. NASA says its twin, Voyager 2, should reach the same distance in November 2035.

Deep dive

Anthropic Begins Trading After the Biggest IPO on Record

What happened. Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on Wednesday, above its expected range, CNBC reports. It sold about 980 million shares and raised $81.3 billion. That tops SpaceX's record June offering. The Claude maker begins trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the new ticker symbol ANT.

Why it matters. The AP reports the price values Anthropic at $1.5 trillion. Shares were set to debut sometime after the market opened at 9:30 a.m. EST. Employees will be able to sell vested stock. The AP cites worries about a possible regulatory crackdown by a Democratic-led Congress next year.

What each side says. The pricing is "a big win for the tech sector," Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives said, according to the AP. The AP notes Anthropic has $6.2 billion in debt and is unprofitable under standard accounting rules. The company says it has deals requiring $900 billion in spending on computing and infrastructure.

What comes next. CNBC reported that perpetual futures indicated a 25% pop at the open, as of 10:30 a.m. ET. The Nasdaq was up 0.6% and the S&P 500 0.1%. Alphabet rose almost 3% and Amazon under 1%. Both have invested in Anthropic. Plancorp strategist Callie Cox cautioned that hot IPOs rarely perform well right out of the gate.
