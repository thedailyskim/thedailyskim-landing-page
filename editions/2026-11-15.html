The big read

Officials Blame a Contractor Router for the Nationwide Flight Shutdown

Reuters reports officials said a single router installed by contractor L3Harris may have caused Friday's nearly five-hour aviation shutdown. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the router flooded other routers with more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds. Officials have not yet determined the fault.

CNBC reports nearly 6,500 US flights were canceled Saturday as of 9 p.m. ET, after more than 9,000 on Friday. Al Jazeera reports the FAA expects full schedule integrity by the end of Sunday. FAA chief Bryan Bedford said he saw zero evidence of anything nefarious.

Judge Blocks Trump From Closing a Historic Washington Golf Course for Now

A federal judge on Saturday blocked the Trump administration from closing East Potomac Park and its public golf course. AP reports Judge Ana Reyes ordered a two-week delay to any work, a day before the course was due to close for 15 months. The order runs through Nov. 28.

NBC News reports the paths and course must stay open to the public while the case moves forward. Reyes doubted the government's safety argument, noting crews cut down more than 400 trees within 10 days. The Justice Department said planning for a world-class course can continue.

SpaceX Catches a Returning Starship in Its Launch Tower Arms

AP reports Starship was caught in midair by the arms of its launch tower on Saturday, about 22 hours after liftoff. It was the first capture of a Starship flying back from space. The booster exploded shortly after Friday's launch, so SpaceX missed its hoped-for double catch.

SpaceX says the ship deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites after reaching orbit. The booster's boostback burn ended early due to low fuel mass, and the cause is under investigation. NASA is paying billions for Starship to land Artemis astronauts on the moon later this decade.

Zelenskyy Says the US Asked About Trading Donbas for NATO Membership

NBC News reports US negotiators floated Ukraine withdrawing from Donbas in exchange for joining NATO, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. He said it was the first time talks had linked NATO membership to territory. The idea came up after the latest round of peace talks in Abu Dhabi.

AP reports Zelenskyy said Kyiv would not surrender its territory and would not recognize occupied land as Russian. He said a full package of security guarantees, military strength and recovery funds could go to a national referendum. He also rejected neutrality, saying it would require a constitutional change.

What Losing Measles Elimination Status Means for American Families

What happened. CBS News reports the United States has lost its measles elimination status, a decision by the Pan American Health Organization. The country recorded more than 5,400 cases this year, more than double last year's total. It is the first time since 2000 that the country missed the standard. PAHO plans a news conference on Tuesday.

Why it matters. The CDC has counted 5,412 cases, more than 590 hospitalizations and at least six measles-related deaths this year. Reuters reports Wisconsin confirmed the death of a second child linked to its outbreak. Pennsylvania reported its seventh death, an unvaccinated Montour County resident. The state has recorded 1,542 cases in 46 counties.

What each side says. CBS medical correspondent Dr. Celine Gounder said unvaccinated people are now at risk of exposure. She said doctors should consider measles in anyone with fever and rash. Epidemiologist Dr. Omer Awan called the loss frustrating and heartbreaking. He noted the MMR vaccine is 97% effective at preventing measles, CBS reports.

What comes next. PAHO says keeping vaccination at 95% or higher is the best way to maintain elimination. Regaining the status requires no local spread or outbreaks lasting more than a year. Gounder noted states set school vaccine rules, and most legislatures reconvene in January. CBS reports nine states made opting out easier in 2025.
