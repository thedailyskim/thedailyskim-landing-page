Honda and Toyota Recall About 1.6 Million Vehicles Over Takata Airbags

Honda and Toyota are recalling about 1.6 million vehicles over a risk that Takata airbag inflators could rupture, Reuters reports via NBC News. Both automakers said no ruptures, crashes or injuries are tied to these vehicles.

Honda said it will recall 810,592 U.S. vehicles from model years 2012-2013 and replace the inflators for free. These inflators contain a moisture-absorbing desiccant, unlike earlier recalls. Honda said the recall covers vehicles sold or registered in 14 states and territories.

ICE Arrests Two People on a Seattle Jet Bridge as Holiday Travel Ramps Up

Masked immigration officers detained two people on a crowded Seattle airport jet bridge on Saturday, the AP reports via NBC News. The Los Angeles-bound Alaska Airlines flight was delayed. Alaska said ICE gave no advance notice during a busy pre-Thanksgiving weekend.

DHS said the operation targeted a Mexican man who allegedly overstayed his visa, CBS News reports. DHS said a second passenger hurled hot coffee at officers and will face assault charges. Gov. Bob Ferguson called the video horrific and chilling, ABC News reports.

A Russian Strike Hits Kryvyi Rih as Drones Hit a Ship Near Finland

A Russian strike on Kryvyi Rih killed two people and wounded 60, local officials said Saturday, the AP reports via NBC News. A 7-year-old boy was among the dead. About 25 apartment buildings and a hospital were damaged, the city chief said.

A cargo ship bound for Kaliningrad was struck by drones in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday, Russian and Finnish authorities said. Reuters reports via NBC News that it caught fire and drifted into Finnish waters. Finland then tightened security in the area.

The Kansas City Current Win Their First NWSL Championship

The Kansas City Current beat the Washington Spirit 2-1 on Saturday night for their first NWSL title, the AP reports via NBC News. Vanessa DiBernardo scored directly off a corner kick in the 54th minute.

Debinha added a goal deep in stoppage time, CBS News reports, and Trinity Rodman pulled one back for Washington in the 98th minute. Washington has lost in the final the last three seasons, the AP reports.

How Mexico Captured One of the FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives

What happened. Mexican forces captured cartel boss Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as El Chapo Isidro, the AP reports via NBC News. The firefight in Sinaloa state left at least 11 people dead. Mexico's Defense Ministry said seven alleged cartel gunmen and four soldiers were killed. He was flown to Mexico City on Saturday.

Why it matters. Meza Flores was one of the FBI's 10 most wanted fugitives, ABC News reports. The U.S. had offered up to $5 million for information leading to his arrest. Authorities allege he leads the Beltran-Leyva organization, aligned with the Mayos faction of the Sinaloa Cartel. The AP calls his capture a blow to that cartel.

What each side says. Mexico's defense secretariat said elite agents were attacked with gunfire and explosives, CBS News reports. Mexico said intelligence from the Trump administration helped, but gave few details, the AP reports. The DEA commended Mexican authorities. Residents of Guasave were left terrorized by heavy gunfire and burning trucks, the AP says.

What comes next. Meza Flores and two lieutenants were placed with Mexico's organized crime prosecutors, CBS News reports. He is subject to a U.S. extradition warrant on drug conspiracy charges, the secretariat said. Violence spread in parts of Sinaloa as word of the operation spread. The cartel is also fighting a rival faction for control.
