The big read

Congress is up for grabs today, and so are 145 ballot measures

All 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governor's races are on today's ballot. Republicans hold a 53 to 47 Senate majority and a narrow edge in the House. More than 57 million people voted early.

Voters will also decide 145 ballot measures, including abortion questions in Missouri, Idaho, Nevada and Virginia, voter ID proposals in several states, and redistricting questions in Missouri, Maryland and Colorado.

Netflix now wants to pick your next show based on your mood

Netflix began rolling out "AI Discover" on Tuesday, a tab that suggests titles based on your mood, how much time you have, or what's trending.

Viewers pick themes like "something light" or "scare me," then refine or start over. The test starts with English language viewers in the U.S. on supported TVs and expands in the coming weeks.

CrowdStrike is spending nearly $500 million to guard against rogue AI

Cybersecurity company CrowdStrike is buying two AI security startups for about $495 million combined, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Fiddler AI, roughly $400 million, monitors AI agents inside companies. Dreadnode, about $95 million, tests AI models and apps for flaws before launch. The deals show businesses adopting AI faster than their security can keep up.

Apple will make dating apps and AI companions 18+ only

Apple told developers on Monday that dating apps and apps with romantic features, including AI companions, must carry an 18+ rating in the App Store, even when those features are only one part of the app.

Apps that share a user's real world location must also disclose it. Starting Nov. 16, developers must add new content descriptors so age ratings match each app's features.

Deep dive

Obamacare sign ups open as 2027 premiums climb about 13%

What happened. Open enrollment for 2027 marketplace coverage began Nov. 1 in most states. A KFF analysis puts the average benchmark silver premium increase at 13% for 2027, after a 26% jump this year. HealthCare.gov sign ups run through Jan. 15, 2027. Shoppers must pick a plan by Dec. 15 for coverage starting Jan. 1.

Why it matters. Benchmark premiums have risen about 42% from 2025 to 2027, according to KFF. Enhanced federal tax credits have expired, so subsidies again stop at 400% of the poverty level. Paid marketplace enrollment fell 12% to 19.2 million by February 2026. The Congressional Budget Office projects enrollment will fall to 13.1 million in 2027.

What each side says. A CMS fact sheet says more than 75% of HealthCare.gov buyers can get plans at or below $50 a month, with about 100 plans each to choose from. KFF counts 11 insurers leaving at least one state. Ohio State's Simon Haeder told The National News Desk shoppers face a "double whammy."

What comes next. The Dec. 15 deadline applies to Jan. 1 coverage, and Jan. 15 sign ups start Feb. 1. California's exchange stays open until Jan. 31. Caps on repaying excess tax credits are gone starting with the 2026 tax year, so the first affected tax filings arrive in early 2027. That could mean surprise bills for some households.
